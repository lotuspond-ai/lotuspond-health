The same operations for every system.

Each operation is one typed contract with FHIR R4 output, whichever system answers it. Writes that touch clinical records wait for a clinician’s approval.

How access works.

LotusPond connects with the access you already have. It never goes around a system’s sign-in, its security checks or its vendor’s terms.

  1. Your access, not ours

    Every connection runs on your organisation’s contracted or delegated access to a system. An adapter for a system is built only with a customer who has written authority to use it.

  2. One key for each use

    A key lists the operations it may call. It is shown once, only a fingerprint of it is stored, and it can be revoked at any time.

  3. Every call on the record

    Each call, allowed or refused, is logged with the organisation, the key, the operation and the outcome.

# Who this key belongs to, and what it may call
curl https://health.lotuspond.ai/v1/organisation \
  -H "Authorization: Bearer lpk_…"

# Run an operation
curl -X POST https://health.lotuspond.ai/v1/operations/claims.status \
  -H "Authorization: Bearer lpk_…"

An operation outside the key’s list is refused, and so is one that only a person can authorise. Until an operation’s first adapter is live it answers 501 operation_not_offered.

People’s records stay theirs.

LotusPond Health also keeps records for people and families. The two sides never mix.

  • Separate authority. An organisation’s key never reaches a person’s own records, and a person’s consent never lets an organisation act in their name.
  • Sharing only when they say yes. With LotusPond Link (planned), your app can ask a person to share records from their LotusPond Health account. They approve it in their consent centre, and can revoke it there.
  • Kept apart. Organisations and their keys live in their own database, never in a person’s records.

Where we start.

India has no dominant hospital system, so the order follows reach: one connection that reads many systems, then the systems the large private chains run.

  • ABDM. Consented records from any ABDM-certified system through one FHIR R4 contract, including the public hospital systems medical colleges are integrating.
  • Hospital information systems. Adapters for writes and operations, starting with Akhil Systems Miracle and InterSystems TrakCare. One adapter usually reaches every hospital in a chain.
  • Insurers and TPAs. Claim status and pre-authorisation, through NHCX where the payer is on it.
